# Gravity

The creative spec of Mohammad AlOulabi's portfolio show at [oulabi.dev](https://oulabi.dev): what each of the twelve
figures shows, what the visitor can do in it, its lines and its ink. In the code, the timeline contract is
`src/lib/journey.ts`, the ink bridge is `src/lib/anchors.ts` and the visitor's outcomes are `shared.play` in
`src/gl/shared.ts`. The words that ship are in `src/content/site.ts` (the lines, in English and Arabic) and
`src/content/ink.ts` (the ink); where they differ from the copy quoted below, those files are what the page says.

## The spine

**You bring the point. I bring the gravity.**

The point the visitor makes with press-and-hold is their idea. They follow that one light from spark to sky. Every
figure shows something gravity does to it, and gravity is Mohammad, a tech manager. He never appears on screen;
the visitor sees only what he holds together. No job words, no CV in the show: the role lives in the physics and in
his first-person lines.

The lines move a story; they never describe the picture. Every figure is caused by the one before it, and the same
light (yours) runs through all of them.

## Voice

First person, short, warm, a little wry, honest about the job. Each figure has one or two lines in Newsreader italic
with the Arabic beneath (Alexandria, gold). The ink notes are his notebook: small, lowercase, English, dry. The record
after the show says what he does in plain words.

## The twelve figures

Each figure: what you see, what you can do (always optional: untouched, it plays out on its own as you scroll), the
lines (EN / AR), the ink, and where your point is.

### Act I, The point

**Fig. 1, The point (النقطة).** Dust drifts and swirls around the cursor under the real sky over Riyadh.
*Hold:* it is pulled into one blinding point under the finger. Release bursts it: the big bang. *Click* (or tap): the
sky is drawn in quickly and bursts smaller, still beautiful. One small light stays marked: yours.
The gate: the page waits on this first screen until the visitor does one or the other (the ring says "hold, or click,
to begin", "tap to begin" on a touch screen, and the music starts with it); scrolling does not move on before that,
and a scroll attempt is answered calmly (the ring comes forward with a soft pulse, the dust leans toward the pointer).
A click or a tap that wanders a little is still a click, a hold that settles after a drift is still a hold, and
nothing but the visitor's own hold or click starts the music there. Skip the show, the fast lane, a deep link and find
in page always go through.
Lines: "Every idea starts as a point." / "كل فكرة تبدأ بنقطة." Then, after the burst: "This one is yours." / "وهذه نقطتك."
Ink: a fine crosshair and the hold ring; after the burst, a leader line to your point labelled `yours`.

**Fig. 2, A hundred versions (مئة نسخة).** The burst cools into the gold and ember nebula. *Drag* stirs it.
Line: "Share an idea, and it turns into a hundred versions of itself." / "شارِك فكرة، وستصير مئة نسخة منها."
Ink: tiny version tags scattered on clumps (`v.2`, `v.17`, `v.64`…), your point still labelled.

**Fig. 3, The center (المركز).** Three bright knots glow in the nebula. One is loud (bright, flickering, light);
the heaviest is quieter and bigger. *Click* the one you think is heaviest: the others fall into it and a sun ignites
there. Untouched, the heaviest wins on its own. The center you pick stays the center for the rest of the story.
Lines: "My first job is finding its center." / "مهمتي الأولى أن أجد مركزها." Then "Not the loudest part. The heaviest."
/ "ليس أعلى ما فيها صوتاً، بل أثقله."
Ink: a circle and a mass label on each knot (`m 0.6`, `m 2.4`, `m 1.1`), `loudest` on the bright light one; after the
choice, `this one` and arrows from the others into it. Your point falls in and settles on a close orbit.

### Act II, The gravity

**Fig. 4, Gathering (الالتفاف).** The disk spins up and planets condense out of it, each a different colour and
speed, arriving on messy orbits. *Drag* turns the system.
Line: "Then people gather around it." / "ثم يلتفّ الناس حولها."
Ink: names in the margin like sketches of who is in the room: `engineering` (the ember world), `product` (the gold
world), `data` (the ringed giant), `operations` (the ice world), and on the world: `who it's for`. Dashed orbits.

**Fig. 5, Alignment (الاتساق).** Two orbits cross and the planets head for a near miss. *Hold* to deepen the
center's pull: the paths separate into clean rings. Untouched, they sort themselves out, slower, and the ink adds
`would have got there. took longer.` (held: `pulled together.`). Nothing ever collides.
Lines: "Everyone moves at their own speed." / "لكلٍّ سرعته." Then "That's fine, as long as they share a center." /
"ولا بأس، ما دام المركز واحداً."
Ink: the two crossing paths highlighted, a small `crossing` mark between them, then `aligned`.

**Fig. 6, The window (النافذة).** The planets drift onto their marks along one launch trajectory, drawn in ink
through all of them, ending at the world.
Lines: "Some days everything lines up." / "في بعض الأيام يصطفّ كل شيء." Then "Those are the days you launch." / "وتلك أيام الإطلاق."
Ink: the dashed trajectory, a `window` bracket, `T−3 T−2 T−1` ticks.

**Fig. 7, Gravity assist (مساعدة الجاذبية).** Your point launches from its close orbit and slingshots around each
team planet in turn, faster at every pass, then falls toward the world. The camera rides with it. *Click* to launch it
yourself; the ink grades the timing (`right on the window.` or `a little early. the planets made up for it.`).
Lines: "Borrow a little pull from everyone," / "خذ من كلٍّ شيئاً من جاذبيته،" then "and it goes further than any of us
could alone." / "تصل أبعد مما يصل إليه أيٌّ منا وحده."
Ink: speed ticks along the trail, real numbers from the motion (`v 11 → 19 → 28 → 42 km/s`), `+8` at each flyby.

### Act III, The world

**Fig. 8, Theirs (لهم).** Your point falls onto the world's night side and splits into city lights that spread and
wire together. *Drag* turns the world a little.
Line: "Then it isn't ours anymore. It's theirs." / "ثم لا تعود لنا. تصبح لهم."
Ink: `first light` at the impact, a quiet count of cities as they light.

**Fig. 9, The storm (العاصفة).** A storm sweeps in from deep space and the lights flicker. The world's field comes up
with it, always there and breathing: the storm bends around it along the field lines and pours into the poles as
aurora, and the field strengthens on its own. *Hold* to push back: the storm speeds up and the field pushes back
harder (its lines tighten and brighten, more of the storm is turned aside, the aurora brightens); let go and both ease
back to normal. Either way no light is lost.
Lines: "Something always breaks at 2 a.m." / "دائماً هناك ما ينكسر في الثانية فجراً." (if it is really between 1:30 and
3:00 for the visitor, add "Like now." / "مثل الآن.") Then "The job is that nobody notices." / "والمهمة ألّا يلاحظ أحد."
and "Sometimes it's even beautiful." / "وأحياناً يكون ذلك جميلاً أيضاً."
Ink: a timestamp `02:07`, the storm front, the field arcs, `lights lost: 0`.

**Fig. 10, The flock (السرب).** The lights lift off into a murmuration that follows the cursor. From the middle of
the figure it stops needing you: it keeps flying in step on its own, and if you chase it, it parts around the cursor.
Hints: *move* ("Lead them" / "قُدهم"), then *let go* ("Let go" / "اتركهم").
Lines: "At first, they follow you." / "في البداية، يتبعونك." Then "Then they don't need to." / "ثم لا يحتاجون إلى ذلك."
Ink: a dashed tether from the cursor to the flock, `following`; later the tether breaks: `on their own.` A margin note:
`best thing I build: people who don't need me in the room.`

### Act IV, Again

**Fig. 11, Again (من جديد).** The camera pulls back until the whole system is one star, and fifty-odd others light
up around it in a galaxy of motes. *Hover* a star to see which platform it is (the nine projects and the also-shipped
list in `src/content/site.ts`); *click* jumps to it in Work.
Lines: "Then you do it again." / "ثم تعيدها من جديد." Then "Fifty-something times so far." / "أكثر من خمسين مرة حتى الآن."
Ink: a circle on your system's star, `this one`; a label on the hovered star (name, sector, role).

**Fig. 12, The sky (السماء).** The galaxy settles into the real sky over Riyadh tonight (star trails and all). Your
point is one of the stars, and it is still there on a return visit. *Drag* turns the sky.
Lines: "You never see gravity." / "الجاذبية لا تُرى." Then "Only what it holds together." / "يُرى فقط ما تجمعه."
Then the journal is signed: his name drawn in ink, Arabic and English (محمد العلبي, Mohammad AlOulabi, `tech manager,
riyadh`), and the last line: "Every idea starts as a point. Bring me yours." / "كل فكرة تبدأ بنقطة. أحضر لي نقطتك."
with his email. "Keep your sky" (the souvenir PNG) stays as a small option. Then the record: Work, Skills, Experience.

## How it is presented

- **A journal over the universe.** The 3D scene is the universe; over it, fine ink draws itself like an astronomer's
  notebook (Galileo's moon sketches, Kepler's plates): circles, dashed orbits, tick marks, leader lines, small
  margin notes. The ink is pinned to 3D objects through `src/lib/anchors.ts` and moves with them. It is his thinking
  about the universe, never a UI.
- **Ink style.** Hairlines (1 px, 0.8 px on dense marks), warm paper white `#EEF2F8` at 55-75% with gold `#E9B866`
  for the key mark of a figure. Lines draw on with stroke-dashoffset as their figure plays (scrubbed, so scrolling
  back undraws them), with a slight hand-drawn wobble (a few px of noise baked into each path, never animated jitter).
  Notes in a small mono (IBM Plex Mono, 11-12 px, lowercase, letter-spaced), numbers tabular. Never more than five ink
  marks at once; ink never covers the line of copy.
- **Each figure is numbered** in the margin: `fig. 5 — alignment` (small mono), the lines below it in the serif.
- **Gestures are optional and never block**, after the first one. When there is something to do, a small ring near the
  cursor shows one verb (hold, drag, click, move, let go, hover). Doing it makes the story yours; not doing it loses
  nothing. The one exception is fig. 1's: the visitor's own hold or click lets the light go, and the show waits for it.
- **Your point is the thread.** One marked light you can always find, from the hold to the sky (anchor `yours`).
- **The side spine.** Twelve ticks on the right edge, the current figure's name beside its tick, click to jump.
- **Natural scroll only.** No snapping, no scroll that moves by itself (`src/lib/scroll.ts`). Each figure's length is
  `FIG_SCREENS` in journey.ts.

## Lovely details

- The center you pick stays the center for the rest of the story.
- "Like now." after the 2 a.m. line when it is really around 2 a.m. for the visitor.
- The ink measures real things from the simulation (the probe's speed, the alignment angle, lights lost).
- The flock parts around the cursor if you chase it once it is free.
- A return visit: your star is still in the sky.
- Reduced motion, the static tier or no JavaScript: the journal as still plates (a still per figure with its ink and
  lines), like a printed book, then the record.

## Look and budgets

Palette: ink `#06070B`, gold `#E9B866`, star `#EEF2F8`, ember `#FF6A3D` (the bang and hot things), sandstone
`#C9A47A`. Aurora: a cold green-teal core with a gold upper fringe, kept quiet (it is beauty, not a rave).
Budget: 60 fps on an M1 at DPR 1.5 on the high tier (≤ 12 ms per frame on the heaviest figure); the watchdog drops a
tier when frames run long. No allocations per frame. Every new effect has a medium and a low path, and a still for the
flow layout.
